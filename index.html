A research direction

My work follows one question: how can a robot better understand and support its user?

I build this answer as a closed loop: read the person, understand the task context, and assist through a physical system.

  1. 01
    Infer intention

    Robust EMG, FMG, and EEG sensing.

  2. 02
    Read the scene

    RGB-D perception and visual task context.

  3. 03
    Share action

    Human-centred control validated on hardware.

Next direction

I am eager to extend robust multimodal sensing into learning-enabled policies on real robotic hardware.

Selected research

From human intent to physical assistance.

Three projects show the same research arc: a real system, the user experience it creates, and the sensing that makes it dependable.

01

Flagship physical system · Shared autonomy

Physical system TNSRE · under review

Vision-Guided Shared Control for a Full-Arm Prosthesis

A full-arm prosthetic system that combines EMG commands, RGB-D scene perception, and shared autonomy.

EMG triggers the user’s intent. Visual context supports autonomous execution for coordinated reaching and grasping. The system is evaluated as a complete human–robot interaction loop, not only as an offline model.

EMG trigger → RGB-D context → shared action
Evidence
8 able-bodied participants + 1 transhumeral amputee participant
Outcome
Shorter completion time and higher success than EMG-only control
Role
Co-first author · framework development and evaluation
02

User-centred evaluation · Physiological human factors

Human Factors and Neural–Muscular Coordination in Prosthetic Interaction

Manuscript in preparation · co-first author

Better task performance is not enough if the user remains overloaded.

This work measures task performance, NASA-TLX, sEMG, EEG, and corticomuscular coupling while people use the full-arm system. It asks how shared control changes both performance and the human cost of control.

Methods
NASA-TLX · sEMG · EEG · corticomuscular coupling
Preliminary evidence
Less sustained activation with intermittent recovery during vision-guided control
03

Robust intent sensing · HD-sEMG

Adaptive Signal Recovery for Reliable HD-sEMG

Published · First author · IEEE TIM 2026

When sensing becomes unreliable, the robot should not lose the user.

I developed an adaptive low-rank recovery framework that reconstructs mixed-channel corruption and motion interference before downstream intention decoding. It makes HD-sEMG a more dependable input for assistive interaction.

Robustness
Mixed-fault evaluation up to 49%
Recognition
95.5% accuracy across seven hand-gesture tasks
