Heng JinResearch portfolio · 2026

A research direction

My work follows one question: how can a robot better understand and support its user?

Useful assistance starts before a robot moves: it needs a dependable estimate of intent, an understanding of task context, and evidence that its help lowers rather than shifts the user’s effort. I study this chain as one physical human–robot loop.

  1. 01
    Make sensing reliable

    Robust HD-sEMG recovery before interpreting intent.

  2. 02
    Guide action with context

    EMG intent and RGB-D context in shared control.

  3. 03
    Measure the human impact

    Task performance, workload, and neural–muscular coordination.

Next direction

Next, I want to build multimodal robot interfaces that combine EMG-based intent with visual task context for reliable shared control. My goal is to carry this loop onto real robot hardware, where learning-enabled assistance can be tested in demanding manipulation tasks and evaluated with the people who use it.

Selected research

From a dependable signal to meaningful support.

The research path is deliberate: make human intent reliable, turn it into context-aware assistance, then measure its value to the person using it.

01

Foundation · Robust physiological sensing

Published · First author IEEE TIM · 2026

Adaptive Signal Recovery for Reliable HD-sEMG

Reliable support starts with a reliable reading of intent.

HD-sEMG can be disrupted by faulty channels and motion artefacts. I developed an adaptive low-rank recovery method that repairs corrupted recordings before intent decoding, giving downstream interaction a more dependable signal.

HD-sEMG → adaptive recovery → reliable intent decoding
Robustness
Evaluated under mixed-fault conditions up to 49%
Recognition
95.5% accuracy across seven hand-gesture tasks
Role
First author · method development and validation

02

Context-aware assistance · Vision-guided shared control

Under review · Co-first author IEEE TNSRE · 2026

Vision-Guided Shared Control for a Full-Arm Prosthesis

EMG signals intent; RGB-D provides task context for coordinated assistance.

This full-arm prosthesis combines EMG commands, RGB-D perception, and shared autonomy for reaching and grasping. It tests the complete physical interaction loop rather than only an offline model.

EMG intent → RGB-D task context → shared assistive action
Physical validation
Real-hardware prosthesis evaluation, including a transhumeral amputee participant
Outcome
Shorter completion time and higher success than EMG-only control
Role
Co-first author · framework development and evaluation
03

User-centred evaluation · Physiological human factors

Manuscript in preparation · Co-first author

Human Factors and Neural–Muscular Coordination in Prosthetic Interaction

Better control matters only if it also reduces the user’s effort.

We compare control strategies with task performance, NASA-TLX, sEMG, EEG, and corticomuscular coupling to examine whether improved assistance also reduces user burden.

Methods
NASA-TLX · sEMG · EEG · corticomuscular coupling
Preliminary observation
Less sustained activation with intermittent recovery during vision-guided control
Role
Co-first author · study design and analysis
